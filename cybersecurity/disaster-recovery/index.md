## What Is a Disaster Recovery Plan?

Even the most fortified, expertly crafted IT infrastructures can crumble during a disaster, leaving you unable to access your essential documents, applications, and software. The longer this disaster renders your network unusable, the more likely you will drain your bank account, lose key clients, and eventually shut your doors for good.

IT disaster recovery is the crucial process of restoring your network and recovering lost data following an accident. With a comprehensive disaster recovery plan, you can restore critical business functions as quickly as possible and minimize financial damage to your business.

Many businesses skip out on disaster recovery plans because they assume a disaster will never strike their business. However, just because you’re not in a hurricane zone or tornado alley doesn’t mean a different type of disaster won’t take down your business. An IT disaster can take many forms, and failing to prepare for every scenario can have long-lasting consequences for your future. Luckily, BL King Consulting can help you [create an exhaustive recovery plan](/cybersecurity/) for various disaster scenarios, ensuring business continuity.

## Examples of IT Disasters

When people picture a disaster, they often think of hurricanes engulfing buildings or power outages turning entire cities dark. While natural disasters can wipe out your IT infrastructure, they aren’t the only threat to your business. Other IT disasters include:

- **Cyberattacks**: When cybercriminals hack into your network, they can wreak havoc on your applications and data. You may end up with corrupted files, deleted data, or an inaccessible network that will only be released if you pay a six-figure ransom.

- **Human error**: Even the most tech-savvy employees can fall for a fabricated email or forget to save their data. One mistake could turn into a major data breach or information loss event.

- **Theft**: Let’s say your employee saves a week’s worth of essential data on their computer, forgets to [back it up to the cloud](/cybersecurity/training/), and leaves their work laptop in an unlocked car. When they return, their computer is gone. Neither you nor your employee can recover the information, and you’re about to miss the biggest deadline of your business career because of it.

- **Hardware or software failure**: Your data exists on servers, hard drives, and other physical devices. If those devices fail, you lose your data. Additionally, a software glitch could erase unsaved files and wipe stored files from existence.
