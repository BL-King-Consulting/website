Get certified before you bid

Some contractors get certified ahead of a bid so it is not a condition standing between them and the award. Others wait until a clause actually shows up in a contract they have won. Both are common, and CMMC-in-a-Box works the same way either way — the only difference is timing, not the work itself.

You inherit an environment, you don't build one

You inherit our compliance environment instead of building your own. Most of the work is done before you are involved. The environment is configured and delivered; your policies get written against how your business really runs.

An expert in your corner, not a dashboard

When the assessor asks how a control is met, we answer it. The assessment itself is performed by a C3PAO, independently, and we sit with you through it. After that, you keep working. We stay on the phone.

Either way, you have two or three people. Nobody is a full-time IT person. And the quotes you have seen run into six figures for something that does not make you a single dollar.

How it goes

Sixty days or less for most small teams.

  1. Week one

    We look at what you have and what your contract actually requires. Not everything triggers Level 2, and some contractors are told they need more than they do.

  2. Weeks two through six

    The environment is configured and delivered. Your policies get written against how your business really runs.

  3. Week seven onward

    You keep working. We stay on the phone.

Who it is for

Two to five people. Sometimes up to ten. Construction, landscaping, machining, fabrication — real trades, doing real work for the Department of Defense.

You hold a DoD contract that requires CMMC Level 2, or you are bidding on one and want certification in hand before you submit instead of racing to get it after you win. Either position works. There is no IT person on staff. The owner or the office manager handles technology alongside everything else.

If that is not you, say so and we will tell you honestly. Larger teams are usually better served a different way, and we would rather send you to the right answer than sell you the wrong one.
